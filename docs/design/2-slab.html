2 · DEPTH: WEIGHT

Slab

Depth expressed as weight rather than lightness. Two fills, but the boundaries are heavy: 1px hairlines for detail and a hard 2px edge wherever something is a distinct object. Hi-vis yellow-green on graphite, which is a machine-shop palette rather than a software one.

The type ramp is the loudest of the four — 700 for structure, uppercase mono for every label — and radius is 3px, small enough to read as a milled edge rather than a card.

What this language buys

  • Object boundaries are unmistakable. In a 4×4 grid the question "where does this pane end" is answered by a 2px edge, not by two greys 4% apart — this is the language that scales best to a full grid.
  • The focused pane is obvious without a glow or a shadow: its edge goes accent. Today focus is a border 10% lighter, which is invisible in peripheral vision.
  • Hi-vis on graphite is nobody else's palette. Yellow-green at chroma 0.20 is a hazard colour, not a brand colour, and it is as far from the current AI-tool violet as the wheel allows.
  • Heavy labels survive at 10px. Uppercase mono at 500–600 stays legible where a 400-weight sans label goes mushy.

What it costs

  • 2px edges eat 4px per pane per axis. Across a 4×4 grid with gaps that is real estate, and it is spent on borders.
  • It is loud. Everything shouts a little: heavy edges, heavy labels, a hazard accent. Six hours in this may be tiring in a way Sheet is not.
  • Yellow-green is close to the success green. They are 18° apart and both high chroma, so "the accent" and "it worked" can be confused at a glance — the accent may need to move to ~135 or the success colour to a blue-green.
  • Warm greys fight the cool semantic colours. Hue 95 graphite under a 200-hue tool colour is a deliberate tension, and on a poorly calibrated display it just reads as muddy.
