7B · T3 CODE, BY THE NUMBERS

Measured

T3 Code's own numbers, not an impression of them. Radius 0.625rem with controls at 0.5rem; hairlines as alpha rather than a grey step — 6% of the ink colour, 10% on inputs; one 52px topbar instead of a 44px header plus a 28px pane bar; the transcript on a 768px column; surfaces as computed lifts of the canvas. Read from their index.css via docs/research/T3-DESIGN-RESEARCH.md §1.2.

Chrome stops being mono. Group headings, control labels and hints are sans in sentence case; monospace is kept for what is actually code. That single change removes most of what reads as «terminal app».

What this treatment buys

  • It is the ask, measured. Nothing here is a guess about what modern looks like — it is the reference's geometry with our palette in it.
  • Alpha hairlines stop the boxes shouting. A 6% edge separates surfaces without drawing a grid over the whole window; regions read as areas, not cells.
  • A 768px column makes prose readable and gives the transcript a shape at any window width — today it is whatever the pane is.
  • One 52px bar replaces two. Fewer horizontal rules, more room, and the title finally has somewhere to live at a real size.

What it costs

  • It costs vertical and horizontal budget. 30px controls, 16px between messages and 20px thread padding is a lot of space to spend when four panes are on screen — see the grid screen before judging.
  • Alpha borders are weaker in light mode. 6% of a dark ink on paper is close to invisible; the light pass leans on fill differences that the dark pass gets for free.
  • A 768px column wastes a wide pane. On a 1400px window in single-pane, roughly half the width is margin — deliberate in a chat app, arguable in a work tool.
