Elevation is deleted. Not reduced from six steps to three — removed. There is one ground and one well for machine text, and every boundary in the application is a hairline. Nothing is lighter because it is nearer.

What is left to carry hierarchy is type and space, so both do more work: a real weight ramp, wide tracking on small caps labels, and radius zero on anything the machine produced. Colour appears roughly eight times on this screen and every appearance is load-bearing.

What this language buys

  • Two fills instead of six. Every surface decision collapses to "is this machine text or not", which is a question with an obvious answer — no more picking between panel and raised for things that are neither.
  • It cannot look like a shadcn app, because the thing that makes those look alike is stacked greys with soft radii, and neither survives here.
  • Colour recovers its force. There are eight coloured marks on this screen and each one means something; today amber and cyan appear so often they have stopped being signals.
  • Square machine surfaces, soft human ones — radius carries meaning instead of being a global constant nobody chose.

What it costs

  • Hairlines are fragile. On a cheap panel or at 100% scaling a 1px 27% rule can disappear, and this language has nothing behind it — no fill difference to fall back on. It needs a real check on a bad monitor before it is committed to.
  • Nothing can be emphasised by lifting it. Popovers, menus, tooltips and dialogs all float above content and today say so with --float. Here they need a border and a shadow, which is the one place the language has to break its own rule.
  • Weight does the work type at 11px cannot always do. Archivo at 600 and 400 is a clear ramp at 13px and a much subtler one at the 10px chrome labels use.
  • Least warm of the four. It reads as an instrument, which is right for a debugger and colder than some people want to sit in for six hours.
