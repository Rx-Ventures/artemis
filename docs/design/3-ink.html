The one thing the agent produces that is genuinely prose is set as prose: Newsreader at 14.5px with real leading, in a measured column. Everything else — chrome, labels, status, code — stays mechanical, in Inter Tight and IBM Plex Mono.

The bet is that a harness is mostly a reading surface and today it treats agent explanation as UI text at the same size as a button label. Surfaces recede almost to nothing so the reading has somewhere to happen.

What this language buys

  • The agent's explanation stops being chrome. At 14.5px with 1.62 leading a paragraph is something you read; at today's 13px UI text it is something you skim and lose.
  • Size tells you what kind of text it is before you read a word: large and serif is someone talking, small and mechanical is the machine. No colour needed to make that distinction.
  • It is the calmest of the four by a distance, and a harness is a thing you sit in all day.
  • Measured column at 62ch — today a wide pane sets prose at 120 characters, which is past the point where your eye reliably finds the next line.

What it costs

  • A serif in a developer tool is a real risk. It is the most likely of the four to be wrong, and it is the choice most likely to divide people on sight. It is also the closest thing here to round one's Ledger, which was rejected.
  • Three faces to load and maintain against today's two, with an optical-size axis on the serif that has to be set correctly or it looks wrong at small sizes.
  • Mixed baselines. Serif prose next to mono output means two different x-heights in one column, and the rhythm needs tuning that a single-family system gets for free.
  • Big prose costs vertical space — the thing every other bet in round two was trying to recover.
