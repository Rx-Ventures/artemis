Five ways the mark turns

Same mark, same disc, same fixed frame — only the motion curve differs. None is a linear spin: every one accelerates and decelerates, and three of them hold still at points, which is what stops it reading as a loading throbber.

Judge them at real size (22px, right-hand cell) — that is where they live. The 56px cell is only so you can see what the curve is doing. Slow motion stretches the same curve rather than substituting an easier one, so what you pick is what ships.

01Escapement

Eight 45° steps per revolution. Each one snaps across, overshoots a hair, and settles — then holds still for slightly longer than the move took. A clock's escapement: work arriving in discrete bites rather than a continuous grind.

8 × 45° · move 5% / hold 7.5% · cubic-bezier(.3,.9,.35,1.25)
Cost: the busiest of the five. Eight events a cycle in the corner of your eye is a lot when you are trying to read the transcript above it.

02Flywheel

One revolution, heavy at both ends: it takes real time to get going, whips through the middle, and coasts to a stop just as the next turn begins. Nothing ever quite stops, so it reads as continuous effort with mass behind it.

360° per cycle · cubic-bezier(.85,0,.15,1) · 2.6s
Cost: the least eventful. At 22px the slow ends can look briefly frozen, which is the one thing an idle must never do.

03Breath

Two half-turns a cycle. Each accelerates hard out of stillness, decelerates into the opposite phase, and rests there for a beat before going again. Because it stops at 180°, the mark lands on its own silhouette both times — it never looks like a shape mid-blur.

2 × 180° · move 38% / rest 12% · cubic-bezier(.55,0,.15,1)
Cost: the rest is 12% of the cycle — about 370ms — and a pause that long can read as "it finished" for the fraction of a second before it moves again.

04Hunt

Drives forward past where it was going, backs off thirty degrees, then drives again. The only one that moves against itself — which is what makes it read as searching and correcting rather than executing a known path. Given what the app is, that is the most honest description of what is happening.

0 → 200° → 170° → 390° → 355° → 360° · four curves per cycle
Cost: reversal reads as hesitation, and hesitation reads as trouble. Fine for "thinking", wrong if it is still spinning while something is actually stuck.

05Surge

A long, barely-moving drift — 48° over more than half the cycle — then a fast 270° sweep, then drift again. The most extreme ratio of the five, and the one that most looks like a long think followed by a decision.

drift 48° over 58% · sweep 270° over 18% · drift 42° over 24%
Cost: the drift is slow enough that a still frame looks stopped, so anyone glancing at exactly the wrong moment sees a frozen app. Mitigated only by the elapsed counter beside it.

What the disc being half-filled does to a spin

This mark is not rotationally symmetric — it is a half-filled circle, so a full turn passes through every phase and reads as a moon cycling as much as a wheel turning. That is why where a profile stops matters. Breath stops at 180°, landing on the mirrored silhouette, so both rest states look deliberate. Escapement stops at eight positions, four of which are diagonal and read as neither phase. If the resting frames matter to you, Breath is the only one that got them for free.

If you want a recommendation

Breath, then Escapement. Breath is the one that feels alive rather than driven — the acceleration is steep enough to be unmistakable at 22px, and it lands on a clean silhouette twice a cycle. Escapement is the better answer if you want the idle to feel like a machine getting through work rather than something thinking, and it is the most obviously never-stuck of the five because something visibly moves eight times a cycle.

I would not ship Surge or Flywheel without a change: both have stretches slow enough that a glance can catch them apparently stopped, which defeats the entire purpose. Surge's is over a second at real speed.

Not decided here

What the mark does at the ends — how it starts when a turn begins and how it resolves when the turn lands. A spinner that simply vanishes is a missed beat; settling to rest at 0° or completing to 360° is a nicer finish and is worth designing once a profile is picked.
