Five ways the shuttle runs

Back to Shuttle for the activity indicator — the bar that grows from the left edge and then leaves from the left edge. Same rule, same bar, five motion curves. None is linear: every one accelerates and decelerates, and the two that matter most also break the fill into uneven beats.

The mark is settled: Terminator. It is no longer doing the waiting, so the two are now independent — the mark can be redrawn without touching the indicator, and this page can change without touching the icon.

The flaw worth fixing. The original Shuttle filled at a constant rate, which is exactly what a determinate progress bar does — so for the first half of every cycle it looked like real progress toward a known end, and then threw it away. Stutter and Handoff are the two that actually solve that; the others only soften it.

01Eased

The minimum honest fix. Grows decelerating out of nothing, sits full for a beat, then leaves accelerating. Nothing clever — it just stops being linear at either end, which is enough to make it feel driven rather than metered.

grow 46% · hold 10% · exit 44% · 1.9s
Cost: still a monotonic fill, so it still resembles a progress bar for the first half of every cycle. Softened, not solved.

02Whip

Nearly three quarters of the cycle is a patient build, then the bar is gone in a flick. The asymmetry is the character: it reads as something accumulating and then being spent, which is close to what a turn actually does.

grow 72% · exit 28% · cubic-bezier(.85,0,.95,1) on the exit
Cost: the slow build is the longest stretch of near-constant motion of the five, so it is the one most likely to be mistaken for a stuck progress bar at a glance.

03Stutter

Grows in three uneven bites — 42%, then 27%, then the last 31% — holding briefly after each, then leaves clean. Because the rate is not constant, the eye stops reading it as a measurement and starts reading it as work landing in pieces, which is what is actually happening.

3 bites · 16/40/66% · holds of 8–10% · exit from 76%
Cost: the holds are short enough to read as jank rather than intent on a display that drops frames. It is the variant most sensitive to a busy main thread.

04Overrun

Arrives at the right edge with enough speed to compress against it, springs back, then releases from the left. One elastic beat and no more — enough to say there is force behind this without turning into a bounce.

grow 48% · compress to 7% inset · release · exit from 64%
Cost: the compression happens at the right edge, which in a narrow pane is 6px of travel — the effect is nearly invisible where most panes live and only really reads at full width.

05Handoff

Two bars half a cycle apart: the tail of one is still leaving as the head of the next arrives, so the rule is never empty. That fixes the original's real defect — a dead beat once per cycle where nothing at all was moving — and it is the only variant that can never be caught looking finished.

2 bars · delay −50% · grow 40% · exit 40% · 2.6s
Cost: two bars on screen at once can read as two things happening, which is wrong — one turn is running, not two. It is also the busiest, and in a 2×2 that is four rules with eight bars between them.

What changed by going back to Shuttle

  • The mark and the indicator are now independent. Phase tied them together — pick a different logo and the idle had to be redrawn. Shuttle has no such coupling, so Terminator can be revised on its own.
  • It survives width. A 22px mark is the same size in a full-width pane and a 2×2 cell; a rule takes whatever width it is given, so in a single pane it is 700px of unmissable motion and in a 2×2 it is still proportionate.
  • It sits where the next turn will appear rather than beside the text, so the eye is already in the right place when output starts.

If you want a recommendation

Stutter. It is the only one that fixes the actual problem rather than dressing it — a bar that fills at three different rates cannot be read as a measurement of anything, so it never makes a promise it throws away. Handoff is the better answer if the priority is that the indicator can never be caught looking still, but paying for that with a second bar means the indicator says "two" while one turn runs, and that is a small untruth in the same family as the ones we have been removing.

Still not decided

What it does at the ends — how it appears when a turn starts and how it resolves when output arrives. Vanishing mid-cycle is a missed beat; letting the current bar finish its exit before the first token renders is a nicer finish and costs one state flag. Worth doing once a variant is picked.
